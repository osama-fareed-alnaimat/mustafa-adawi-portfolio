Manual Double Roll Bending Machine

A complete SOLIDWORKS model and drawing set for a hand-operated sheet-metal roller

The challenge

Turn a set of specifications into a full drawing package

A manual double roll bending machine curves sheet metal by feeding it between two rollers. Turning the handle drives a pair of gears that rotate the rollers in opposite directions, pulling the sheet through. The brief was to read the orthographic drawings of every part, model them in SOLIDWORKS, assemble the machine and calculate its total mass.

All drawings had to follow ISO standards in millimetres, with sectional views, numbered balloons, a bill of materials, an exploded view and a full title block. Identical parts were modelled once and reused, and any missing details such as fillets and chamfers had to be chosen sensibly.

My role

What I did

  • Modelled all 22 parts in SOLIDWORKS: frames, stands, rollers, rods, gears, handles, knobs, bolts and nuts.
  • Assembled the machine with mates that match how the real parts fit and move.
  • Specified materials for each part: gray cast iron for the structure, Nylon 101 for the lever parts and knobs, and teak for the handle.
  • Produced an assembly drawing with numbered balloons and a bill of materials.
  • Detailed each component in third-angle projection, with dimensions, thread callouts such as M8×1.25 and M14×1.5, and part weights.
Design process

From part to drawing set

  1. Model each part

    Built every component as its own 3D part from the given dimensions. The gear-side frame, for example, is a 160 × 135 mm block with two Ø39 mm slots for the roller shafts, a Ø24 mm hole and threaded holes for its fasteners.

  2. Assemble the machine

    Combined the parts into a full assembly: the rollers and rods between the two frames, the gears and control handle on the drive side, and everything mounted on two triangular stands.

  3. Detail every component

    Produced a separate A3 drawing for each part in third-angle projection, with orthographic and isometric views, full dimensions, thread callouts, material and weight in the title block.

  4. Assembly drawing and BOM

    Finished with an isometric assembly drawing: 22 numbered balloons linked to a bill of materials that lists each part, its material and its quantity.

Results

  • Complete 3D model of the machine with all 22 parts assembled.
  • Assembly drawing with balloons and a bill of materials.
  • Detailed third-angle drawings for the individual components, including dimensions, threads, materials and weights.
  • Total mass of the assembled machine calculated in SOLIDWORKS from the assigned materials.

Lessons learned

  • A clear part list from the start makes a large assembly much easier to manage.
  • Small parts such as bolts, nuts and knobs need the same care in drawings as the main frames.
  • Consistent drawing standards make a set of sheets read as one professional package.
