Project 03

Dual-Axis Solar Panel Tracking System

A sensor-driven tracker that keeps a solar panel facing the sun all day

The challenge

Follow the sun, automatically

A fixed solar panel only faces the sun directly for a short part of the day, so it loses energy in the morning and afternoon. The goal was a low-cost tracker that senses where the light is coming from and turns the panel on two axes to follow it.

The system had to combine mechanical, electrical and control parts. These were a frame strong enough at every tilt angle, a safe power supply, and Arduino logic that reads four sensors and drives two motors.

My role

What I did

  • Developed the electrical connection plan for the whole tracker.
  • Planned power distribution between the solar panel, charge controller, battery, 300 W inverter and DC-DC converter.
  • Defined the Arduino connections for the four LDR sensors and two servo motors.
  • Supported integration of the electrical and control components into the frame.
  • Built and tested the prototype together with the team.
Design process

Sense, decide, move

  1. Design the frame in SOLIDWORKS

    The team modelled the base, panel holders, panel support and sensor head in 3D and produced an assembly drawing with a bill of materials. AISI 1045 steel was selected for the structural parts for its strength and suitability.

  2. Check the structure with FEA

    Ran static studies at different operating angles (0°, 45° and 86°) to make sure the frame stays safe across the full tilt range. The peak von Mises stress was about 71 MPa, far below the 530 MPa yield strength, a safety factor of roughly 7.4.

  3. Plan the electrical system

    I created the connection plan. Power flows from the solar panel through a charge controller to the battery, a 300 W inverter feeds an output socket, and a DC-DC converter supplies the Arduino and servos. The four LDRs and two servo motors are mapped to specific Arduino Uno pins, with a common ground.

  4. Integrate, build and test

    The Arduino compares the light on opposite LDR pairs and moves the servos until the readings balance, so the panel points at the brightest direction. Expected solar efficiency and energy production were estimated for environmental conditions in Bahrain.

Results

  • Detailed electrical connection plan covering power supply, sensor inputs, motor control and interconnections.
  • Frame validated with FEA at 0°, 45° and 86° tilt: peak stress about 71 MPa against a 530 MPa yield strength.
  • Working dual-axis prototype that follows a light source on both axes.

Lessons learned

  • A clear connection plan before wiring saves time and prevents damage to components.
  • Mechatronic projects only work when the mechanical, electrical and software parts are designed together.
  • Checking a moving structure at several positions, not just one, gives real confidence in the design.
