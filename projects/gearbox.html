Three-Stage Spur Gearbox Design

Speed reducer for an airport baggage conveyor system

The challenge

Reduce speed, increase torque, fit it all in one housing

An airport baggage conveyor needs high torque at low speed, but the drive motor runs at 1,920 RPM. The task was to design a gearbox that reduces this to about 160 RPM (a 12:1 ratio) while transmitting a design power of 41.24 kW reliably.

A single gear pair would need a very large wheel for this ratio, so the reduction is split over three spur gear stages. Every gear, shaft and bearing then has to fit inside a compact housing without parts touching each other.

My role

What I did

  • Selected the spur gears for each of the three reduction stages.
  • Calculated shaft sizes for the loads transmitted at each stage.
  • Chose materials for the gears, shafts and housing.
  • Modelled every part and the full 3D assembly in SOLIDWORKS.
  • Found and fixed interference between components so the assembly fits together.
  • Produced exploded views and engineering drawings with dimensional tolerances.
Design process

From requirement to drawings

  1. Define the requirement

    Started from the conveyor duty: 1,920 RPM input, about 160 RPM output and 41.24 kW design power. The overall 12:1 ratio was split across three stages of 3:1, 2:1 and 2:1, using gear pairs with pitch diameters of 100/300 mm, 120/240 mm and 120/240 mm.

  2. Select gears and size shafts

    Selected the spur gears for each stage and laid out the four shafts at centre distances of 200, 180 and 180 mm (560 mm overall). Shaft diameters were sized from the transmitted torque, rising from Ø20 mm on the input to Ø50 mm on the output, and each shaft is carried on cylindrical roller bearings.

  3. Model and assemble in SOLIDWORKS

    Built every gear, shaft, bearing cover and housing half as a 3D part, then combined them in a full assembly with mates that reflect how the real gearbox is put together.

  4. Check interference and produce drawings

    Ran interference detection on the assembly, corrected the clashing components, then produced dimensioned engineering drawings and an exploded view with a 37-item bill of materials covering gears, shafts, keys, circlips, bearings, covers and fasteners.

Results

  • Completed 3D gearbox assembly delivering a 12:1 reduction (1,920 → 160 RPM) at 41.24 kW.
  • All assembly interference issues found and corrected, so the components fit together.
  • Full set of engineering drawings and an exploded view with a 37-item bill of materials, ready for manufacture.

Lessons learned

  • Splitting a large ratio over several stages keeps gears compact and easier to make.
  • Interference checks in CAD catch fit problems that would be expensive to fix after machining.
  • Clear tolerances on drawings are what turn a 3D model into a part a workshop can produce.
