The challenge

A frame that is strong, light and buildable

A golf cart chassis carries the passengers, batteries and drivetrain, and it takes the loads from the suspension over uneven ground. It must be strong and stiff enough to do this safely, light enough to keep the vehicle efficient, and simple enough to fabricate in a university workshop.

The chassis team also had to work closely with the suspension team. Their mounting points and loads directly affect the frame design.

My role

What I did

  • Designed 3D CAD models of chassis and suspension components in SOLIDWORKS.
  • Ran finite element analysis (FEA) to check structural performance and find high-stress areas.
  • Improved the strength-to-weight ratio and selected suitable materials.
  • Produced engineering drawings, a bill of materials (BOM) and project documentation.
  • Worked with the suspension team during the manufacturing phase.
  • Built the design into a working physical prototype.
Design process

Model, analyse, build

  1. Model the chassis in SOLIDWORKS

    Created a tubular space-frame model of the chassis in SOLIDWORKS: a triangulated centre cell for the floor and batteries, a front section for the steering and suspension mounts, a rear section and a roll hoop behind the seat.

  2. Check the structure with FEA

    Ran static studies in SOLIDWORKS Simulation to find high-stress areas and adjust members for a better strength-to-weight ratio. For the front suspension A-arm, the factor-of-safety study gave a minimum FOS of 3.2. Load case used: [ADD VALUE].

  3. Document, cut and weld

    Produced engineering drawings and a bill of materials, then cut and fitted the steel tubes. Parts such as the A-arm were laid out and checked before welding.

  4. Build the prototype with the suspension team

    Worked with the suspension team in the workshop to manufacture and assemble the chassis, turning the CAD design into a working physical prototype.

Hands-on

In the workshop

Cutting, turning, drilling and fitting the parts myself, from steel tube to finished joint.

Results

  • Chassis designed in SOLIDWORKS and validated with FEA before fabrication. The suspension A-arm reached a minimum factor of safety of 3.2.
  • Working physical prototype built and presented on campus.
  • Complete drawings and BOM delivered as project documentation.

Lessons learned

  • FEA is most useful early: it shows where material is needed and where it can be removed.
  • A design that works on screen still has to suit the tools and skills available in the workshop.
  • Regular communication with the suspension team avoided mounting and fit problems during assembly.
